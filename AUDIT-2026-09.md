# Independent audit, September 2026 — disposition

An independent audit of FigureLab v3.13.0 raised eighteen findings (FL-1 … FL-18).
This file records what happened to each one. Everything is dispositioned; nothing is
left implicit.

Released as **v3.13.1** — a patch bump, because the work is entirely correctness and
disclosure. No feature was added and nothing new is possible that was not possible
before; what changed is that several things that were wrong are now right.

**Two findings reached files that go to journals.** If you have exported a figure with
a journal column width selected, or pasted a generated caption into a manuscript,
re-export and re-read them — see FL-1 and FL-2.

## Fixed

### FL-1 — printed width now reaches the exported bytes (P0)

`renderExportCanvas` tagged every export with `Math.round(scale × 96)`. That is the
true resolution only when no printed width is set; with one set, the scale is
`(mm/25.4) × dpi / logicalW` and multiplying it by 96 gives a number related to
nothing. A figure targeted at 183 mm and 300 DPI was written as **210 dpi, 261 mm** —
43 % oversize and below the ≥300 dpi bar the compliance panel had just reported as
cleared, while the on-screen readout said "183 mm, 300 DPI" the whole time. Every
column preset was affected; the error scaled as `dpi × 25.4 × lw / (96 × mm)`, so
89 mm at 600 dpi was 168 % too large.

With a target width the physical size is the user's choice and the resolution follows
from the pixel count, so `_effDpiForPixels` derives it that way and falls back to
`scale × 96` only for free width. It equals the requested DPI exactly when uncapped,
and correctly reports the reduced DPI when the ~60 MP cap fires. The cap's own toast
was wrong for the same reason and is fixed with it. The canvas now also carries the
scale it was rendered at, which FL-3 needs.

*Tested by reading the bytes:* the PNG `pHYs` chunk, the TIFF `XResolution` rational
and the PDF `/MediaBox`, over five column-width presets from 55 mm to 183.5 mm, each
required to land within 0.5 mm of its target. The free-width path is asserted
unchanged.

### FL-2 — the caption reports µm/px, not an invented magnification (P0)

The legend printed `1/(µm per px)` as "×". That is sampling density; it has no
relationship to the objective. A 100× oil lens on a 6.5 µm camera pixel gives
0.065 µm/px, which was captioned "(15×)". Nothing FigureLab knows can yield the true
magnification, so it now states the quantity it has. `_insetMag()` and the
`linkedInset` log entry are a genuine *relative* magnification of an inset against its
parent and are deliberately untouched.

### FL-3 — panel crops use the scale the canvas was rendered at (P1)

`panelBounds` are logical units; the export canvas is rendered at
`_exportScaleFor(dpi)`. Cropping it with `Math.max(1, dpi/96)` reads the wrong
coordinate system under a column width or the 60 MP cap. On a four-panel figure at
300 dpi and 183 mm, panel 2 was cropped at x=2100 of a 2161 px canvas — 877 px past
the right edge, so the file was blank — and panel 3 came out 43 % too wide, taking in
its neighbour. `exportPPTX` cropped by the same wrong rule and is fixed with it (see
*Deviations*).

### FL-4 — the caption discloses every adjustment applied (P1)

Levels were disclosed and nothing else. Gamma, brightness, contrast, invert, the LUT
and background keying all reached the pixels undeclared — and gamma is precisely the
non-linear adjustment Nature, JCB and Cell require in the legend. Every adjustment is
now named once (the LUT and grayscale are skipped where the preceding sentence already
says them), and the legend states whether the settings were uniform across panels or
differ, which journals ask separately. Nothing needed new tracking: the data was
already on the panel and already in the CSV and `PANEL_HISTORY.txt`. The predicate is
deliberately the same shape as `_panelHistory()` and `_adjustedPanelNote()`, so the
caption, the lab records and the measurement disclaimers cannot disagree.

### FL-5 — multi-page PDF uses the export canvas and the right page size (P1)

It grabbed the 1× on-screen preview canvas and divided its pixel width by the
requested DPI, so pages were `dpi/96`× too small — 83.7 mm at 300 dpi for a figure
whose single-page PDF was 261 mm — and the printed width was ignored outright. Each
page now goes through `renderExportCanvas` and takes its page size from the resolution
that canvas actually carries. *Deferred:* the audit also suggested offering the
lossless Flate path here; see below.

### FL-6 — a scale bar that cannot be drawn now fails the check (P1)

Out of range (4 px … 75 % of the panel; 60 % in freeform) the bar, its label and its
drag handle were simply not painted, with no toast and no badge, while
`checkCompliance` tested only whether µm/px was set and reported "All calibrated ✓". A
mistyped calibration or unit therefore shipped a figure with no scale bar and a green
report. Both renderers now record the reason, which surfaces as a failing compliance
row, an export pre-flight warning, and a "⚠ no bar" badge in the panel list.

### FL-7 — zero network requests (P2)

The `<head>` fetched a Google Fonts stylesheet on every open, handing Google the IP
and referrer of every lab machine that opened the tool; and the `bi_line` icon's
inline SVG embedded `@font-face{src:url(https://excalidraw.com/Virgil.woff2)}`, so
placing that icon — or opening an SVG export containing it — reached a third party.
Both are gone. The two display faces stay first in their stacks (a font *name* costs
no request), with real fallback stacks behind them; the figure's own labels were never
affected, as they use the label-font control. `tools/build-icons.mjs` now strips
`@font-face` rules on import so the next icon tranche cannot reintroduce one.

Fonts were **not** vendored as base64. System stacks were the other option the scope
allowed, and they are the better one here: no binary payload in a 1.3 MB single file,
no embedded-font licence obligations, and the app already rendered this way whenever a
user was offline — so this makes the offline appearance the only appearance rather
than introducing a new one.

Two tests now guard it: the source contains no fetchable external URL (`<link>`,
`<script src>`, `url()`, `@import`, `img src`, `xlink:href`), and opening the app
issues no non-`file://` request at all.

### FL-8 — SVG honours the printed width (P2)

`exportSVG` never called `_exportScaleFor`, so its declared size was always
`logical/96` inches: choosing a column width changed PNG, TIFF and PDF and silently
did nothing here. `width`/`height` now come from the target; the `viewBox` stays in
logical units, so the vector layer is byte-for-byte what it was.

### FL-9 — the one red test on master (P2)

`v35.spec.js` cached `annCanvas.getBoundingClientRect()` once and reused it for every
synthesised event. Clearing the selection removes the freeform context toolbar and
moves the canvas up 31 screen px, so the final mousedown landed at y = 380.347 and
missed the target rect's bottom edge by 0.35 logical px. The app was right —
`canvasCoords()` re-reads the rect live — and the test now does the same. Because the
margin was a third of a pixel and the toolbar's height is font- and platform-
dependent, this could fail locally while CI passed, or the reverse.

### FL-10 — `images[]` restored in a `finally` (P2)

Any throw between the page swap and the restore left the live session holding one
page's slice, with the user's other panels gone and no undo entry for the swap.
Regression test: `renderExportCanvas` is stubbed to throw mid-export and the panel list
and row count are asserted intact.

### FL-11 — the submission ZIP says what it could not build (P2)

`_captureDownload` swallowed every per-artifact error, so a TIFF or lossless PDF that
threw was dropped while the toast still reported success — a gap the journal finds,
not the author. Failures are now recorded with a reason, shown in a modal, and written
into the package's own `README.txt`, which outlives the toast. The PNG and TIFF in the
package also now get their canvas's effective DPI rather than the nominal one,
matching what `doExport` already did.

### FL-12 — compliance megapixels use the real scale (P2)

`exportPx` assumed `dpi/96` two lines after `_physFacts()` had correctly resolved the
target, over-reporting by ~2× under a column width and mis-firing both the "exceeds
~60 MP" warning and its Lower-DPI button.

### FL-13 — scale-bar length is no longer quantised (P2)

The grid rounded the bar to whole *logical* pixels. Everything is drawn in logical
units and scaled once at the end, so the rounding happened before supersampling and
exporting at 600 or 1200 dpi did not reduce the error: 1–2 % on typical bars, +9.2 %
on a 2 µm bar at 0.16 µm/px, up to ~12.5 % at the 4 px floor. Freeform never rounded,
so one calibration drew two different bars depending on the layout mode. The `round()`
is gone. *Deferred:* the range thresholds themselves — see below.

### FL-14 — citation metadata (P2)

The README's prose citation and BibTeX `version` still said 3.6.1, seven minor
releases behind. Both updated, and `encoding.spec.js` now checks the citation block a
reader actually copies, not only the title heading.

**On the DOI question:** the audit's conclusion is confirmed and no DOI changed.
`10.5281/zenodo.21269456` is the **concept DOI** — it always resolves to the latest
version, and it is correctly what the README badge, the prose citation, the BibTeX
`doi` field and the in-app citation use, labelled as such in `CITATION.cff`. The
`…21305142` in the original brief is the **version DOI for v3.6.1**, correctly listed
as one of the per-version identifiers and correctly not used as the badge. The README
no longer hard-codes any single release as "this exact release"; it points at
`CITATION.cff`, where every version DOI lives.

`CITATION.cff` carries a `# DOI-PENDING: 3.13.1` marker, because Zenodo cannot mint a
version DOI before the release commit exists. The follow-up commit that records the
real DOI deletes the marker.

### FL-15 — README export internals (P3)

The section claimed "correct DPI in Photoshop / ImageJ" and a "correct physical page
size" — both false under FL-1 — and described PDF as JPEG-only, long after the
lossless Flate path became the submission package's default. Rewritten to state how
printed width anchors the pipeline, what each format writes, the zero-network property,
and the FL-18 limitation.

### FL-16 — landing page (P3)

`--gold` (#b07c30) is used for body-sized text at 3.43:1 on the page background and
3.13:1 on the warm band, both under WCAG AA's 4.5:1. Darkened to #8a5f1f (5.30:1 and
4.83:1); its decorative uses only need 3:1. Added a skip link — there is a seven-item
nav above every page — and `:focus-visible` styles, of which the file previously had
none. The three GitHub cards took their lift from `onmouseover`/`onmouseout` alone and
now have a keyboard equivalent.

### FL-17 — `.zenodo.json` (P3)

The description had drifted back to the v3.9-era feature set while `CITATION.cff`'s
abstract stayed current, and `.zenodo.json` is what Zenodo shows on the record page.
It is now derived from that abstract, with the keyword lists in step, and a test holds
them together.

## Documented, not fixed

### FL-18 — PDF text metrics (P3)

`_pdfTextOps` measures each label with a canvas context set to the *browser's* font and
uses that width to convert centre/right alignment to a left origin, then renders the
glyphs as non-embedded base-14 `/Helvetica`. Helvetica's advances differ from SF Pro or
DejaVu by a few percent, so a centred label can sit roughly 1 pt off centre.

Not fixed, deliberately. The honest fix is to measure with real base-14 metric tables,
which means embedding ~1400 advance widths across the four faces used — a meaningful
payload in a single-file app, to correct a sub-point placement error in a label that is
correct in content and selectable as text. The competing approach, embedding a font,
would end the "no bundled binaries" property that makes the decision not to embed a
good one in the first place. The audit's own assessment is that the base-14 + WinAnsi
design, which leaves unrepresentable glyphs (Greek, ≥, ✓, CJK) in the raster rather
than substituting them, is the right call for scientific labels.

It is now stated in README's Technical Notes and in `PUBLICATION_QUALITY_NOTES.md`, so
it is a known trade-off rather than a silent one.

## Deferred

**Lossless multi-page PDF (part of FL-5) — done after the v3.13.1 release.** At
release the page size and supersampling were fixed but the multi-page writer was still
JPEG-only at q 0.92, while the single-page path offered a `/FlateDecode` branch. It was
deferred because it was a second, independent PDF byte-writer, and plumbing the Flate
branch and the vector text layer through it was a change of a different size from the
correctness fix. The follow-up removed the second writer instead of teaching it the
same tricks: `exportPDF` and `exportMultiPagePDF` now share `_pdfPageFor` and
`_pdfAssemble`, and a single-page PDF is a one-element page list. The multi-page
export is lossless by default (a checkbox beside "Rows per page" restores JPEG) and
lays every page's labels down as selectable text. Byte-level tests cover both modes,
and `tests/validate_pdf.py` parses the multi-page file with pypdf.

**Scale-bar range thresholds (part of FL-13).** 4 px, 75 % (grid) and 60 % (freeform)
are inconsistent with each other, and whether any of them is the right number is a
judgement call for the author, not an audit finding — the audit says so explicitly.
The bug was the *silence*, which FL-6 fixed: an out-of-range bar is now reported with
its reason rather than vanishing. The numbers are left as they are.

## Deviations from the audit's proposals

- **`exportPPTX` was fixed alongside FL-3.** It is not in the finding, but it is the
  same wrong crop rule thirty lines away, and leaving one of two identical bugs fixed
  would guarantee the other came back.
- **The submission package's PNG and TIFF now use their canvas's `_effDpi`.** Not in
  FL-1's text, which covers `doExport`; the package passed the nominal DPI, which was
  right under a column width but wrong whenever the 60 MP cap fired.
- **FL-7 was solved with system font stacks rather than vendored base64 faces.** Both
  were authorised; the reasoning is under FL-7 above.
- **`encoding.spec.js`'s DOI test now accepts a declared pending state.** The repo's
  release workflow is a release commit followed by a "Record the … Zenodo DOI" commit,
  and the test failed for the window between them — by design, but it meant the suite
  could not be green at the release commit. The test now accepts an explicit
  `# DOI-PENDING: <version>` marker, and fails if that marker is stale, names the wrong
  version, or is simply absent for a released version with no DOI.

## Out of scope, untouched

No image-processing behaviour changed. Brightness, contrast, gamma, levels, LUTs,
invert, background keying and the crop pipeline produce exactly the pixels they
produced in v3.13.0 — this release changes what is *said* about them, not what is done.
`archive/` was not touched.
