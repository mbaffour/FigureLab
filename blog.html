Why I built this

Assembling a figure panel is one of the most tedious parts of publishing. You export images from ImageJ, montage them in PowerPoint or Illustrator, fight with scale bars and panel letters, and then discover the export is the wrong resolution and the journal bounces it.

I wanted a single browser tab that does the whole thing - arrange panels, burn in an accurate scale bar, label A/B/C, and export at the resolution a journal actually asks for. No installs, and your images never leave your computer.

The goal: go from raw panels to a journal-ready figure without leaving the browser - and without a single desk-reject over resolution or a missing scale bar.

What it does

🧩

Multi-panel layout

Arrange microscopy, blot, and gel panels on a canvas with alignment and spacing controls.

🖨️

Journal-grade export

Export at 300-1200 dpi as PNG with correct DPI metadata, TIFF, or vector SVG.

📐

Calibrated scale bars

Burn in accurate scale bars from a known micron-per-pixel calibration.

🔠

Panel labels

Consistent A/B/C lettering with control over font, size, and position.

How it works

  1. Add your panels

    Drop in images from microscopy, blots, or gels - straight from your machine.

  2. Arrange and annotate

    Lay them out, add scale bars and panel labels, and adjust brightness/contrast.

  3. Export journal-ready

    Pick the DPI and format your target journal requires, and download.

Private by design

Everything runs client-side - your images are processed in the browser and never uploaded. You can even share a figure layout via a link without sharing the underlying images.
